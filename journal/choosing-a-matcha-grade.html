Ceremonial, latte, culinary: choosing a matcha grade

November 4, 2026

Japan has no legal grading system with names like “ceremonial” or “premium”. These are trade terms, and each supplier uses them a little differently. Treat them as a starting point and let a sample decide.

What the names usually mean

  • Ceremonial: typically first-harvest tea with the brightest green, low bitterness and strong umami. Made to drink with water alone.
  • Premium or latte grade: chosen so that color and tea flavor still come through in milk. This is the usual choice for café lattes.
  • Culinary or food-processing grade: often later-harvest tea with more bitterness and a slightly olive tone. Used in baking and ice cream.

How to test a sample

  1. Look at the dry powder. Fresh, good matcha is a vivid green. Aged or degraded matcha turns reddish or brown.
  2. Drink it with water alone. Whisk 2 g with 60 ml of water at 70–80°C. Check bitterness and aroma.
  3. Make your actual menu drink. Use your own milk, your own ratio and your own cup. Check that the color and flavor hold.
  4. Do the math. Compare the cost per drink, using the grams your recipe really uses.
